Abstract

Over the past decade, neural text-to-speech (TTS) systems have achieved remarkable progress for high-resource languages, primarily driven by open-source speech datasets. However, developing high-quality large-scale multispeaker TTS corpora remains time-consuming and costly, particularly for low-resource languages such as Kazakh, due to the need for professional voice talent and extensive recording efforts. In this work, we expand our previously released KazakhTTS2 speech synthesis corpus using synthetic voices. Compared with KazakhTTS2, the proposed KazakhTTS3 corpus increases the total duration from 271 to 589 hours, and expands the number of speakers from 5 (three female and two male speakers) to 12 (six female and six male speakers). To efficiently scale the corpus, we employ an LLM-based synthetic data generation pipeline using the Orpheus-TTS model. To validate the effectiveness of the KazakhTTS3 corpus, we train lightweight multispeaker Matcha-TTS models and evaluate them using both subjective and objective metrics. Experimental results demonstrate that the model trained on the full KazakhTTS3 corpus achieves the best overall performance, obtaining an average mean opinion score (MOS) of 3.83, a word error rate (WER) of 6.21%, a speaker similarity score (SSIM) of 0.87, and a mel-cepstral distortion (MCD) of 5.32. Furthermore, the model achieves real-time inference on both GPU and CPU platforms while supporting all 12 speakers within a single model. To support future research, we publicly release the corpus, source code, and pretrained models to advance TTS development for Kazakh and other low-resource languages.
